Tab Slop Gauge

A pair of bars that mesh along both long edges. Slide the bottom bar along the top one until the fit feels right: its end points at the slop that suits your laser and material. The bottom bar's tabs also come in different lengths, so you can find the length that sits flush with your real material thickness.

How to read it

  1. Cut both bars from the same sheet. In the SVG, outlines are black, inner cuts are blue and etching is green. Set your laser to cut blue before black, so parts don't shift before their slots are cut.
  2. Turn the bottom bar end for end and mesh its side A with side A of the top bar (or B with B).
  3. The bottom bar is one tab longer than the top bar, so one of its ends always sits on the top bar. Slide it one tab at a time: every pair of tabs gets one increment looser or tighter.
  4. Stop where the bars mesh best. The end of the bottom bar that lies on the top bar sits on a tick: that number is the slop to use. The foot of each tick points along the bottom bar; if it points the other way you have slid past the end of the scale.
  5. The top bar also has a row of slots inside each edge, matching the edge slots along the bar and a little wider than the material across it. Push the bottom bar's tabs into a row from the face instead of meshing along the edge; the shifts and readings work the same way.
  6. To check the thickness, push one of the tabs on the ends of the top bar into the slots across the bottom bar. Those two tabs are the nominal size: the tab width by the thickness. Each slot is as wide as its label says; the narrowest one the material slides into snugly is your real thickness.
  7. For the tab length, stand the bottom bar upright and push its tabs through the slots of the top bar. The tab that ends flush with the far face shows the length that matches your material.

The SVG has no kerf compensation, so the slop reading includes your laser's kerf.
